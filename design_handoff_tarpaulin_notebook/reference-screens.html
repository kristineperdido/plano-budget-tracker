TURN 3 · A DIFFERENT ANSWER
The store's
handwriting
…not its pixels.
Pixel art fought your numbers — a game font can't set ₱15,086 and stay readable. So here's the other whimsy: what a sari-sari store actually looks like. A hand-painted tarpaulin nailed over the counter. Prices in marker on a ruled notebook. Masking tape holding things down. Tally marks because nobody's counting in a spreadsheet. It's warm and funny and hand-made — and it lets every amount stay in clean tabular mono, because in real stores the numbers are the one thing written carefully.
THREE LAYERS
Tarp for anything shouted — headers, totals, the buffer. Marker for anything a person would jot — asides, nudges, empties. Mono for every peso, always.
WHAT'S NEW
Two colours join the palette: tarp teal for structure — ruled lines, eyelets, labels — and a tape blush. Neither ever touches a number, so green/brick/gold keep their meanings intact.
THE WHIMSY RULE
Everything playful is physical — tape, eyelets, tally strokes, a slightly crooked sign. Nothing playful is ever a number, a colour with meaning, or a joke about being broke.
